01Problem Costs here are modelled, not measured. Sheet 03 replaces them with an instrumented baseline before any target is committed.

The report is cheap. Assembling it is not.

A recurring client report is the same nine moves every time: pull GSC, GA4, Semrush, GEO, check what shipped in the CMS, paste into a sheet, compute deltas, write the narrative, format the deck. Eight are mechanical. The ninth — deciding what mattered and what the client should do — is the only part a client pays for, and it is what gets squeezed when the first eight run long.

  • Capacity. At an assumed 12 CSMs × 4h, reporting burns ~48 h/week — about 1.2 FTE of margin, and a hard ceiling on accounts per CSM.
  • Consistency. Every CSM's spreadsheet is a private fork; two clients on one plan get different metrics and different definitions of "traffic".
  • Latency. Reports arrive on a calendar, not on an event — a ranking collapse surfaces at month-end, weeks after it was actionable.

Atlas already holds the connections and the data. The gap is that nothing turns access into a client-ready artifact without a human doing data entry.

03Goals Non-goals are commitments — each is a place we chose not to spend v1.

Goals

  • Cut analyst time per report by ≥ 50%; ambition 80%.
  • Make every number in a client report traceable to a source query.
  • One metric definition per concept, across all clients.
  • Turn CSM edits into a training signal, not lost work.

Non-goals (v1)

  • No auto-send. A human approves every report a client sees.
  • No client-facing chat — Q&A is CSM-facing first.
  • No deck export: web report, emailed summary, PDF.
  • No forecasting or budget recommendations.
  • Auth, billing, dashboard chrome — assumed to exist.
04Solution shape Stages 1–3 are deterministic code. Only stage 4 is a model, and it may speak only about findings stage 3 produced.

A pipeline with a model in exactly one place

1 · CollectPinned pull from bound sources on an as-of date, per-source coverage recorded.
2 · NormaliseOne fact table — entity × metric × period × source. Everything downstream reads only this.
3 · DetectRules, not vibes. Deltas, rank moves, AI-citation gain/loss, publish→impact, each with effect size.
4 · NarrateGrounded LLM writes from findings only. Every sentence carries the metric IDs it used.
5 · ReviewHuman gate. CSM edits in Report Studio, approves, sends. Edits are logged.

The split matters more than any single stage. Arithmetic done by a language model is unverifiable and occasionally wrong in ways nobody sees until a client catches it; arithmetic done in stages 2–3 is unit-testable and cheap. The model gets the narrow job it is genuinely good at — choosing which of forty true findings are the four worth a client's attention, and saying them in the account's voice.

08Grounding A spec, not a guideline — Sheet 03 tests each clause directly.
Non-negotiable — the five clauses

1. No number reaches prose unless it resolves to a metric ID in the fact table.  2. Missing data is disclosed, never imputed — "Semrush ranks unavailable 12–14 Jul; excluded from the weekly average."  3. Every report prints its as-of date, and a re-run of that period is identical.  4. Causal language is gated; the default phrasing is correlational.  5. A human approves before a client sees it.

Clause 3 exists because GSC restates recent days for roughly 48–72 hours: without pinned as-of semantics, the same "last month" report yields different numbers on Tuesday than on Thursday.

11The question Two questions, not one. Time saved alone is gameable: a tool that emits a plausible report in nine seconds saves four hours and destroys client trust in a quarter.

Did it save the four hours — without costing us the report?

Hypothesis

H1. Atlas Reports cuts median CSM time per approved report by ≥ 50% versus the measured baseline.  H2. It does so with no degradation in report accuracy, client engagement, or CSAT.

Baseline first. "4+ hours a week" is the stated problem, not a measurement. Weeks 0–2 run an instrumented baseline — in-app timers plus a one-minute weekly diary — establishing minutes-per-report, its variance, and its split across the nine steps. Every target below is expressed against that number.

15Experiment Randomising by client would leak: a CSM who gets faster on one account gets faster on all of them. Contamination — control CSMs hearing about the tool — is tracked weekly and treated as bias toward the null.

Stepped-wedge, clustered on the CS manager

  • Design — 8 weeks, 4 cohorts of CSMs crossing over every 2 weeks in randomised order. Everyone ends up treated: that protects adoption, gives a within-subject contrast, and separates the effect from seasonality and the month-end peak.
  • Outcome — minutes per approved report, via a pre-registered mixed-effects model minutes ~ treated + week + (1|csm), α = 0.05 two-sided, reported as an effect with its 95% CI rather than a bare p.
  • Power — at between-CSM SD ≈ 25 min and ICC ≈ 0.25, ~14 CSMs × 8 weekly observations gives > 80% power for a 25-minute reduction. Under 10 CSMs the wedge is underpowered — fall back to a within-subject interrupted time series, ≥ 6 pre and ≥ 6 post weeks each.
16Decision Written before the data arrives, so the result is read rather than negotiated. Stated formally in §20.
VerdictPre-registered condition
ShipTime reduction ≥ 50% with CI lower bound > 25%; every quality gate met; no guardrail breach; L2 overall ≥ human baseline − 0.3, accuracy ≥ 4.5/5.
HoldAny quality gate missed, whatever the time saving. A wrong number in front of a client is the one failure that does not average out.
RethinkBy week 8, acceptance < 40% and edit distance not trending down — the insight layer has not earned trust. Fall back to assembly-only: the pipeline builds the data sections, the CSM writes the narrative. That still removes eight of the nine steps.

Known limit — eight weeks measures time and quality, never retention or account growth. Those need a year and more accounts than we have, and are tracked as observational lagging indicators, not evidence of causation.

17Time model Sheet 03 asks whether the four hours fell. This asks which hour fell — the only version of the question that can distinguish success from a CSM rubber-stamping.

Where the four hours actually go

A single minutes-per-report number cannot tell we removed the assembly from the CSM stopped reading, and the guardrails that would catch the second — CSAT, client-flagged errors — are quarterly and rare. So the north star is banded across the nine moves from §01, and each band carries its own target.

BandMoves it coversTargetInstrument
CollectionPull GSC, GA4, Semrush, GEO; check the CMS→ 0Pipeline replaces it entirely
AssemblyPaste into the sheet, compute deltas→ 0Metric layer (FR-3) replaces it
CompositionWrite the narrative, format the deckFormat → 0; narrative → review-and-editStudio canvas time
JudgmentDecide what mattered; decide what to recommendHeld flat or upEvidence-panel + edit time

Instrumentation. The in-app timer already attributes session time by active surface — source panel, studio canvas, evidence panel — which maps onto the bands without asking the CSM for anything extra. The weekly diary supplies one corroborating split. Weeks 0–2 measure the real shape; until then the bands have targets but no baseline.

The shape test

If total time falls but the judgment band falls with it, that is a failure, not a success — we will have automated the thinking and kept the typing. A report that takes 18 minutes with 12 of them spent on judgment is the win. Eighteen minutes with two is the failure mode this metric exists to catch.

18Capacity The metric the business buys. It is second-order here because it physically cannot move inside an eight-week trial.
FacetAccounts per CSM at cadence-compliant quality
In the trialModelled, not measured. Nobody hands a CSM new accounts mid-wedge, so capacity headroom is inferred: time freed per CSM-week ÷ time per account-week. The wedge measures time; capacity is the inference drawn from it, and the document should say so rather than dress an inference as a result.
Post-GABecomes a real tracked metric — accounts per CSM and reports per CSM-week, quarterly, with the expected trajectory pre-registered before GA so it cannot be re-fitted afterwards.
Failure modeCapacity bought with cadence slippage or quality drop. The §14 quality gates travel with this metric, and FR-12 cadence adherence is the check: headroom claimed while adherence falls is not headroom.
Operational SLOTime to first draft ≤ 4 min at p90 (pipeline telemetry). Not a success metric — a latency floor, since a draft that misses the T−72h window costs a CSM the whole review lead.
19Human ceiling §13 compares against the report the CSM sent — a floor. This measures the ceiling, and the distance between two competent humans.

What "human-level" actually scores

StepProtocol
Sample15 of the 60 golden cases from §13, stratified across the same archetypes.
ProcedureThree senior CSMs write each case independently from the same frozen snapshot, blind to one another and to the report originally sent.
Cost~90 min per report per rater ≈ 67 senior-hours, run once, refreshed annually or on a major model change. Stated plainly because a protocol whose cost is hidden is a protocol that never gets run.
OutputsHuman mean and SD per rubric dimension — the ceiling and the noise floor. Inter-rater agreement (ICC or Krippendorff's α). Finding overlap: how often two humans independently pick the same 3–5 findings.
UseThe target stops being "beat the historical report" and becomes "land inside the human band". Where two competent humans disagree, an agent that differs from one of them has not necessarily erred.

It also disciplines §12: a rubric dimension where three senior CSMs cannot agree is a dimension no LLM judge should be trusted on either, and probably not one we should gate a release on. Expect finding overlap to come in lower than intuition suggests — which reframes a good share of "the agent missed something" as ordinary human variance.

20Decision rule §16 gives the verdicts. This gives the test behind them, so the margin is derived rather than chosen after the fact.
EndpointPre-registered test
Quality
Non-inferiority		One-sided. H₀: μ_agent ≤ μ_human − δ against H₁: μ_agent > μ_human − δ, with δ = 0.5 × the inter-human SD measured in §19 — half the noise between two competent humans, rather than the round 0.3 §16 quotes. Accuracy carries a tighter δ and an absolute floor: it is the one dimension where sitting inside the human band is not sufficient.
Time
Superiority		One-sided, from the §15 mixed-effects model. Ship requires the CI lower bound above the stated MDE, not merely a point estimate that clears it.
BothRequired together. Non-inferior quality alone is not a win; faster alone is certainly not. Because two primary endpoints must both pass, this is an intersection–union test and takes no alpha adjustment.
Revealed preferenceAfter the wedge, two weeks optional. Ship needs ≥ 70% continued voluntary use. It prices what the rubric cannot — trust, fit, and whether the thing is pleasant to use.

Everything else is exploratory — segment cuts, per-template breakdowns and archetype-level results are labelled as such when reported. Pre-registration is only worth something if the list stays short.
