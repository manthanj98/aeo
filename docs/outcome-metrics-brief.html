01

Why the four hours is the wrong headline

Four hours saved is worth nothing if the brand reaches its goal no sooner.

A tool that halves reporting time and changes nothing else has moved an internal cost line. That is real, but it is not what a client renews for — and it is trivially gameable, because the fastest report is the one nobody thought about.

The hours only convert into value through two doors: the CSM spends them on the judgment that moves a brand's numbers, and the CSM carries more brands. Every metric below exists to check that one of those two doors actually opened.

The chain, in one line.

"Hours freed become brands served, brands served become goals hit sooner, goals hit sooner become share of voice and citations, and that is what the retainer is judged on."

Four metrics carry the argument. Each needs a definition sharp enough to instrument, or the tier above it is decoration.

04

Time saved, instrumented to the panel

A single minutes-per-report figure cannot tell "we removed the assembly" from "the CSM stopped reading". The in-app timer already attributes session time by active surface, so the breakdown costs no extra telemetry — and each surface means something different when it moves.

Panel 01

Source panel

Time spent pulling and checking raw source data. Falling towards zero is the core win — this is the assembly the pipeline replaces.

Target → 0
Panel 02

Studio canvas

Time editing the drafted narrative. Falling means draft quality is rising; flat after week 4 means the narrative layer is not learning.

Falling = trust
Panel 03

Evidence panel

Time verifying claims against source. Must not reach zero — a CSM who stops checking has not got faster, they have started rubber-stamping.

★ Floor, not target
Panel 04

Approve & send

Time from last edit to decision. The residual, and the cleanest read on whether the CSM trusts what they are about to put their name on.

Residual

The shape test. If total time falls but judgment time falls with it, that is a failure, not a success — we will have automated the thinking and kept the typing. A report that takes 18 minutes with 12 spent on judgment is the win. Eighteen minutes with two is the failure this breakdown exists to catch.

05

How we'd know it actually saved the four hours

The measurement, dataset and success criteria in short — the full design sits on sheets 03 and 04 of the PRD.

01

Measurement

Stepped-wedge, clustered on the CS manager.

Eight weeks, four cohorts crossing over every two weeks in randomised order. Randomising by client would leak — a CSM who gets faster on one account gets faster on all of them.

Analysis is a pre-registered mixed-effects model on minutes per approved report, reported as an effect with its 95% CI. Weeks 0–2 establish the baseline first; "4+ hours" is the stated problem, not a measurement.

02

Dataset

60 golden reports, plus a measured human ceiling.

Twelve clients × five historical periods, frozen as source snapshots and stratified for what breaks things: partial source sets, mid-period replatforms, a flat month, a real incident.

Fifteen of those cases are rewritten independently by three senior CSMs, giving a ceiling and a noise floor rather than just the report that happened to be sent.

03

Success criteria

Faster and no worse — both, or it does not ship.

Time: one-sided superiority, CI lower bound above the stated MDE. Quality: one-sided non-inferiority with the margin derived from measured inter-human SD, not a round number.

Then the gate nobody can game — two weeks optional after the wedge, and ≥ 70% of CSMs still choosing it. Velocity and capacity are read post-GA, over quarters, not weeks.

Faster to goal, across more brands. The hours are how we pay for it.
