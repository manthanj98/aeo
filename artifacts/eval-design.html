Does it actually save the four hours?

§1

Decompose the four hours before measuring them

“Building reports” is seven tasks, and Atlas hits them very unevenly. Measure the aggregate without the split and you can celebrate a 30% saving on the tasks that were already cheap.

Task in the current workflow What Atlas does to it
1Pull and export across five sourcesGSC, GA, citation crawls, backlinks, rank trackingEliminated
2Stitch and normaliseDate-align, dedupe, fold engine-name variantsEliminated
3Build charts and deck, apply brand formattingEliminated
4Write the narrative: what changed and whyPartial
5Internal QA and reviewNow reviewing AI output — new work, not removed workUnchanged
6Send, schedule, follow upReduced
7Ad-hoc re-cuts and “what does this mean?”A live dashboard invites questions rather than settling themMay increase

Rows 5 and 7 are the honest part. A tool that writes the narrative creates a new verification job, and a client with a live dashboard asks more questions, not fewer. Both are pre-registered as things that may move the wrong way, and the headline number is net of them.

§2

Three instruments, and self-report is never the primary

S1 · Atlas telemetry

Ground truth, after launch

Event stream, sessions stitched on a 120 s idle gap, each segment attributed to a report_cycle_id.

S2 · work-surface metadata

The primary baseline instrument

Drive revision timestamps on the client’s sheet and deck, plus email send times, resolved to edit-session minutes per cycle.

S3 · diary & sampling

Calibration only

A 30-second end-of-cycle form and two random pings a day, to catch work that leaves no digital trace.

Why S2 carries the design

It exists retrospectively, so the baseline is reconstructable from the last two quarters without running a study first — and it exists in both periods, so the before/after comparison is not confounded by a change of instrument. That second property is worth more than its precision: an instrument that only exists after launch cannot measure a saving.

Units, fixed in advance

Primary causal unit: the report cycle (client × cadence) — that is what the product acts on. Business claim unit: the CSM-week — that is what “4+ hours a week” means. Both get reported, because they can disagree, and the disagreement is the finding: a CSM whose per-cycle time halves while their book of business grows has created capacity for the company without getting an hour of their own week back.

§4

Offline gates, passed before anyone’s time is measured

A missed insight is a worse report. A wrong insight is a slower one, because the CSM has to go and check it — and a CSM who re-verifies every line has saved nothing. Recall and precision fail for different reasons, so they are gated separately.

Deterministic checks before judges

Numbers in a report are extractable and comparable against the snapshot the report came from. Sending arithmetic to a language model to be graded is slower, costlier and less reliable than comparing two floats. Only the irreducibly fuzzy parts go to a judge — and rules the product already stores as data get enforced at 100% by a linter, with nothing left to judge.

What the stress set is for

Each fixture carries one planted defect: a quoted figure the snapshot contradicts, a competitor the client does not track, an unexpanded acronym, a panel of confident insights about an 11-day-old account. The harness finds exactly those and blocks the rollout. If G1–G3 do not pass, the experiment does not start.

Why not a parallel A/B

A CS team is 10–25 people. At that size a between-person comparison is badly underpowered; within-person contrasts recover most of it. Everyone gets the tool eventually, so there is no months-long control group watching colleagues use the new thing. And a staged rollout is what would happen anyway.

What it costs, and the fix

Treatment is confounded with calendar time by construction — later periods have more treated CSMs. Period fixed effects handle it; that is the stepped-wedge estimator. Randomise CSMs and not accounts: a CSM cannot be half-treated, and the outcome is CSM time.

Committed in advance

Each CSM’s first post-crossover cycle is excluded from the primary estimate and reported on its own line. The first report built on a new tool is slower; folding it in either flatters or sinks the number depending on when you stop measuring. Control is enforced by feature flag and control-arm logins are verified to be zero, not assumed.

§6

The headline test permutes the schedule, it does not trust a standard error

With roughly 15 clusters, cluster-robust standard errors over-reject. Rather than assert that, the design measures it: 300 simulated studies with a true effect of exactly zero, each analysed both ways.

False positives when the true effect is zero · α = 0.05
CR1 cluster-robust Wald 7.7%
Randomisation inference 5.3%
dashed line = the 5.0% a calibrated test should produce · axis 0–10%

CR1 finds half again as many effects as it should when there is nothing there. Permuting the observed crossover schedule across CSMs reproduces the actual randomisation and is exact under the sharp null, so that is the headline p-value. CR1 is still printed, as a cross-check.

And the obvious analysis is not good enough either

On simulated data with a known 35% effect planted, at the realistic team size of 15, the naive pre/post comparison of means recovers 32.5% with a mean absolute error of 0.055; the fixed-effect estimator recovers 34.3% with an MAE of 0.029. On a panel with a true effect of exactly zero, the naive weekly comparison reads as 32% worse — purely because late-crossover CSMs and later calendar periods are not the same thing.

§7

Power, simulated rather than assumed

There is no clean closed form for a stepped wedge with unbalanced clusters, a skewed outcome and an exclusion rule, so the power calculation is the data-generating process itself: 250 simulated studies per cell.

A team of 15 detects a 17% saving, and the ship bar is 50%. The risk is not missing a real win — it is noise manufacturing a fake one, which is what §6 is about.

Tightening the instrument from 0.65 to 0.35 buys more than growing the team from 15 to 25. That is a week of work on the S2 calibration against a hiring round — the highest-leverage thing to do before the study starts.

§8

The decision rule, written down first

50% is not a round number picked for looks: it is what turns “4+ hours a week” into “under 2”.

SHIP
≥ 50% reduction in minutes per cycle, and a 90% CI lower bound ≥ 35%, and ≥ 40% reduction in hours per CSM-week, and no guardrail breach. minutes/cycle down 56.6% (bar 50%) 90% CI lower bound 52.2% (bar 35%) hours/CSM-week down 59.5%: 5.2 h -> 2.1 h
ITERATE
20–50% reduction, or the reduction lands but the interval is wide or the weekly saving is absorbed by a growing book of business. minutes/cycle down 32.9%, inside the iterate band 90% CI lower bound 26.1% below the 35% bar hours/CSM-week only down 37.3% (5.2 h -> 3.3 h) -- per-cycle savings partly absorbed by account load
KILL / RETHINK
< 20% reduction, or any guardrail breach. A CSAT breach overrides any size of time saving — a faster report nobody trusts is not a saving. CSAT guardrail breached (-0.43 < -0.20) -- a faster report nobody trusts is not a saving
Also pre-registered: where did the time go

Freed hours are attributed to more accounts, to strategic work, or to nothing, via the diary instrument and accounts-per-CSM. Without this, “saved 4 hours” is a claim no observation could contradict. The weekly bar is deliberately looser than the per-cycle bar, because time being refilled with more accounts is a real and expected outcome — it should read as ITERATE, not get quietly reported as success.

Every number here is reproducible

Standard library only, Python 3.9+, nothing to install. The estimator is verified by planting known effects and checking they come back out — and by planting none and checking that none is found.
